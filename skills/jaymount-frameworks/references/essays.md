# Jay Mount's essays

The essays live at https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems. Every essay page has a markdown copy at `<url>/index.md` that starts with `Author:`, `URL:`, `Published:` and `Updated:` lines. Use those lines for the citation date. The one-line summaries below are the site's own; read the essay before you quote it.

- [AI Slop Is Human Slop. Where Do You Think It Learned That?](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/ai-slop-is-human-slop): AI slop is low-effort, low-substance content made in volume with a language model. It is also human slop. The model learned to write filler from the filler we published for twenty years, and a person prompted it, skimmed it and shipped it. Take the word AI out of the complaint and see what is left.
- [Buy, Rent, or Build (and With Whom)](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/buy-rent-or-build): Every capability conversation in GTM collapses into build or buy. The question is wrong twice. There are four options, not two. And it skips the axis that decides whether you end up owning anything at all: with whom.
- [The Demand Worth Having](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-demand-worth-having): Content is infinite now. Any piece a competitor could have produced with the same tools is landfill the moment it ships. The demand worth having comes from publishing something only you can publish, then being able to see who showed up for it.
- [The Asset Nobody Had Compiled](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-asset-nobody-had-compiled): The GTM tooling market had no free, neutral comparison layer. Not because nobody wanted one. Because nobody could afford to compile one. Then the cost of compiling collapsed, and I built it. The moat was never the compiling.
- [Most 'AI GTM' Is Just Faster Spray-and-Pray](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/faster-spray-and-pray): The category sold as AI GTM mostly accelerates the old volume playbook. More touches, generated faster, with no new judgment about who deserves one. That is not a new motion. It is the dying motion with a bigger engine.
- [Quantify the Pain or Don't Bother](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/quantify-the-pain-or-dont-bother): Felt pain gets you a meeting. It never gets you a budget line. The fix is not a better ROI deck. It is getting the buyer to compute the cost of their own problem, from their own inputs, before you propose anything.
- [Taste Is the Last Job](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/taste-is-the-last-job): The machine produces almost-good at infinite volume. The one skill left to sell is telling almost-good from good. That skill is trainable, it is encodable, and the operators who write theirs down are the only ones it will keep paying.
- [The New Org Chart: Specify, Execute, Verify](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-new-org-chart): The functional org chart is a machine for organizing scarce human execution. When execution moves to machines, the chart reorganizes around the two functions left: specifying what good looks like and verifying you got it.
- [Build the Gate, Not the Bot](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/build-the-gate-not-the-bot): Every AI line item in your budget is generation, and generation is rented. Your competitors rent the same models. The asset that cannot be rented is the gate: your definition of what good means in your business, encoded into checks that run before anything ships.
- [Calibrate Before You Trust](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/calibrate-before-you-trust): A gate that has never rejected anything is not a gate. It is decoration. Before you trust any system's no, you have to watch it catch something you broke on purpose.
- [The Seams Are Where the Work Is](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-seams-are-where-the-work-is): Every component passed and the meaning still died in the handoff. When machines do the execution, the expensive failures happen between the stages, and they wear green.
- [Verification Is the New Scarcity](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/verification-is-the-new-scarcity): A sharp cold email used to tell you something real about the sender. Now it tells you nothing. When generation gets free, every signal built on effort dies, and the market reprices around the one thing still scarce: proof.
- [The FDE and the GTM Engineer Are the Same Job](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/fde-vs-gtm-engineer): Forward deployed engineers and GTM engineers are the same job arriving from opposite directions. The interesting technical problems moved out of the product and into the customer's world. The org chart just hasn't caught up.
- [You Don't Do the Work Anymore](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/you-dont-do-the-work-anymore): For two hundred years, getting ahead meant doing the work faster. AI ends that. When generation is free, the value moves up to the two things a machine cannot own: deciding what good looks like, and proving you got it.
- [The AI SDR Lie](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-ai-sdr-lie): Every conference is selling AI SDRs as a way to replace your team. That's the lie. AI doesn't replace. It leverages.
- [After the Sequencing Tool](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/after-the-sequencing-tool): I used to think a fourth generation of sequencing tools wasn't coming. I was wrong. The fourth one is coming. It just won't look like the previous three.
- [Fixed Headcount as a Strategic Constraint](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/fixed-headcount-as-strategic-constraint): The most useful constraint a B2B function can adopt right now is to refuse to hire. Without it, AI is just a faster way to do the same work with the same number of people.
- [Context Engineering for GTM](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/context-engineering-for-gtm): Every AI workflow in B2B that survives a model swap shares one trait. It's not the model. It's the context the model has access to before it produces anything.
- [What Replaces the Stack](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/what-replaces-the-stack): Every B2B stack has been organized by category for ten years. That model just broke, and it broke at the seams between the tools. The replacement comes in two forms, and which one fits depends on whether you're building or buying.
- [The CRO Job Is Splitting](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/cro-job-is-splitting): The Chief Revenue Officer role is splitting into two distinct jobs that don't share a path. Most companies haven't named it yet. The ones who notice first are the ones who hire deliberately for what they actually need.
- [Define, Execute, Review](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/define-execute-review): Every AI model is trained on everything, which means it's trained to be average. The way you beat average is the bookends: what you put in before it runs and what you do after.
- [Hire for the Bookends](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/hire-for-the-bookends): My role has quietly shifted toward the edges: more time defining work, more time inspecting output, less time in the middle.
- [AI Isn't Smart. You Are (Or You're Not)](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/ai-isnt-smart): AI is a genius four-year-old. Raw horsepower, no context. What you feed it, and what you do with what comes back, is the entire game.
- [You Don't Have a Pipeline Problem. You Have a Patience Problem](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/pipeline-patience-problem): Everyone wants the pipeline. Nobody wants to wait for it. Teams restart instead of committing, and the restarts are the actual problem.
- [The First Hour](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-first-hour): Most of the diagnosis happens before the first meeting ends. Not because the problems are obvious. Because the patterns are.
- [How You Work Isn't a First Principle](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/how-you-work-isnt-a-first-principle): Your outbound sequence is not a strategy. It's an implementation of something deeper.
- [Going Deep Is the Shortcut](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/going-deep-is-the-shortcut): The first 80% of any motion is table stakes. The part that works (the part people pay for) lives in the details after that.
- [Why Outbound Stopped Working](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/why-outbound-stopped-working): The B2B outbound motion that worked five years ago is producing diminishing returns at most companies. The fix isn't more volume. It's a different model entirely.
- [We've Been Here Before](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/weve-been-here-before): Every few years a tactic saturates and everyone panics. The pattern is always the same. The principles underneath never changed.
- [The New B2B Playbook: What's Actually Working Now](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-new-b2b-playbook): Teams working 40 accounts with deep research outperform those blasting 200.
- [What Comes Before That?](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/what-comes-before-that): Most people stop at the first answer. The ones who get it right keep asking until the question itself hurts.
- [The Intelligence Layer: Why Market Knowledge Wins](https://jaymount.com/essays-on-systems/the-intelligence-layer): Most B2B teams have data but no understanding. The teams that turn data into context (who tell you when to reach out, not just who) pull ahead and don't come back.
